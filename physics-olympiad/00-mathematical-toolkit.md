# 0. The Mathematical Toolkit (IPhO Syllabus: General Problems)

This file is the full reference edition of the math toolkit — read it once end to end before starting `01-mechanics.md`. Every other topic file also inlines the specific book-and-chapter pointer it needs directly in its own tables, so once you're working through a physics topic you should not need to come back here; this file exists for the complete picture and as a place to look something up if a topic file's inline pointer isn't enough on its own.

**A note on chapter numbers:** editions of these books renumber chapters over time. Every entry below gives the exact book (author, full title, edition, publisher, year) plus the **chapter title**, which is what to search your copy's table of contents for — treat the chapter number as a strong hint, the title as the ground truth.

## Dimensional analysis and order-of-magnitude estimation — the IPhO-specific skill this section exists to name

- **Tier 1:** the dimensional-analysis sections of Daniel Kleppner & Robert Kolenkow, *An Introduction to Mechanics*, 2nd ed., Cambridge University Press, 2013 (early chapters), plus Jaan Kalda's methodology notes (see `01-mechanics.md`); practice by re-deriving simple formulas (pendulum period, terminal velocity, orbital period) from dimensions alone before seeing the derivation.
- **Tier 2/3:** Sanjoy Mahajan, *Street-Fighting Mathematics: The Art of Educated Guessing and Opportunistic Problem Solving*, MIT Press, 2010 (published open-access under a Creative Commons license), and Sanjoy Mahajan, *The Art of Insight in Science and Engineering: Mastering Complexity*, MIT Press, 2014 (also open-access) — the closest thing that exists to a graduate-level text on this specific IPhO-relevant skill.

## Spaced repetition for this file

Put every derivation *trigger* (e.g., "eigenvalues → normal-mode frequencies," "divergence theorem → Gauss's law") into Anki as a cued-recall card, not the formula alone — you want the trained reflex of *which math tool a physical situation calls for*.
