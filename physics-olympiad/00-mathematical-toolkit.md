# 0. The Mathematical Toolkit (IPhO Syllabus: General Problems)

This file is different from the others: it isn't "a topic," it's the reservoir every other topic file draws from. Read the master table below once end to end before starting `01-mechanics.md`, then treat it as a reference you return to constantly. Chapter numbers are given for a named edition where known, but editions vary — the **chapter title** is what to search your copy's table of contents for; treat the number as a hint, not a guarantee.

## Dimensional analysis and order-of-magnitude estimation — the IPhO-specific skill this section exists to name

This is not "extra" math — it is a distinct, trainable skill that shows up constantly in IPhO problems (sanity-checking an answer, estimating a quantity with no formula given, solving a problem by dimensional analysis alone when the full derivation would take too long) and that standard calculus/physics textbooks barely teach.

- **Tier 1:** work through the dimensional-analysis sections of Kleppner & Kolenkow's early chapters and Kalda's methodology notes (see `01-mechanics.md`); practice by re-deriving simple formulas (period of a pendulum, terminal velocity, orbital period) from dimensions alone before ever seeing the derivation.
- **Tier 2/3:** Sanjoy Mahajan, *Street-Fighting Mathematics: The Art of Educated Guessing and Opportunistic Problem Solving* (MIT Press, published open-access under a Creative Commons license) and his companion book *The Art of Insight in Science and Engineering* (MIT Press, also open-access) are built for exactly this — they formalize "Fermi estimation" and dimensional reasoning into a repeatable method, and are the closest thing that exists to a graduate-level text on this specific IPhO-relevant skill.

## Note-taking and spaced repetition for this file specifically

Because this file is pure reference material you'll return to dozens of times, put every derivation trigger (e.g., "eigenvalues → normal-mode frequencies," "divergence theorem → Gauss's law") into your spaced-repetition deck (Anki) as a cued-recall card, not the formula itself — you want the trained reflex of *which math tool a physical situation calls for*, which is a different and more valuable memory than the formula alone.
